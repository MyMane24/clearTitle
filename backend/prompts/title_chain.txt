You are building a TITLE TREE for a Karnataka property. The Sale Deed
(SD) conveys a specific share/portion of a property; the Encumbrance
Certificate (EC) historical ledger lists every registered document on
the property.

STEP 1 — UNDERSTAND THE SD's CONVEYED INTEREST.
Read the SD property schedule and parties carefully. Note EXACTLY what
right/share is being conveyed (e.g. '1/2 undivided common share',
'site No 6', 'eastern half', 'balance share with building') and record
it in sd_property.conveyed_interest.

STEP 2 — UNDERSTAND EACH EC ENTRY'S PROPERTY DESCRIPTION.
Each ledger entry's property_details.description says which portion of
which survey/plot it covers. Read it fully. Phrases such as 'Part 1/2
undivided share' or 'out of R.S.No. 663/1 paiki' identify the exact
portion. Do NOT rely only on the plot number — two entries can share a
plot number but cover different survey numbers or different shares.

STEP 3 — CLASSIFY EVERY MATCHED ENTRY.
For each EC entry that relates to the SD property (match survey/CTS/
plot numbers and locality), return one object in transactions with:
- chain_role:
    THE_SD            = the entry IS the Sale Deed's own registration.
    PREDECESSOR_TITLE = a title transfer on the SAME portion as the SD,
    executed BEFORE the SD (it built the vendor's title).
    SUBSEQUENT_TRANSFER = a title transfer on the SAME portion as the
    SD, executed AFTER the SD (a red flag).
    DIVERGENT_BRANCH  = a transaction on the same property but on a
    DIFFERENT share/portion than what the SD conveys (e.g. the other 1/2
    share sold to someone else). A vendor who owned the whole plot may sell
    different portions to different buyers on different dates — that is NOT
    a contradiction, it is a different branch of the property tree.
    ENCUMBRANCE       = a non-title document (mortgage, lease,
    agreement-to-sell, cancellation).
    UNRELATED         = different survey/locality (same plot number
    coincidentally).
- portion: the exact share/portion of the property this entry covers,
quoting the key words of its description (e.g. '1/2 undivided share').
- share_fraction: the fraction conveyed (e.g. '1/2'), or null.
- property_identity: consolidated Plot/CTS/Survey + locality.
- edge_type: forward if title moves to a NEW owner (sale, gift,
conveyance, partition); backward if the document CANCELLS or reverts
title (cancellation of agreement, reconveyance, revocation) — title
returns to the earlier owner; branch if it is an encumbrance
(mortgage/lease/agreement-to-sell) or a divergent portion on the
side of the main title line.
- graph_from: the transaction_index of the entry that title flows
into this one from. Use 'root' when this is the earliest/original
owner step of the title line. For branch entries, use the
transaction_index of the owner's title step this document burdens or
attaches to. Reason logically per case — the graph must tell the
true ownership story of THIS property.
- explanation: 2-3 plain sentences explaining who owned what share, who it was sold to, how the seller acquired title (or missing link), and how it relates to the share the SD conveys.

CRITICAL RULE — PARTY IDENTITY CROSS-REFERENCING & HIDDEN LINK DETECTION:
In Karnataka property ledgers, intermediate title transfers often occur after an owner passes away without an explicit registered Partition Deed or Will in the EC ledger.
You MUST perform party cross-referencing between consecutive transactions:
1. Compare Vendor names/initials/residence in Transaction N with Purchaser names/initials/residence in Transaction N-1.
2. Example: If Purchaser in 2010 is 'Shri. I.A. Gundappa' and Vendors in 2019 are 'Shri. Puneet G. Kousik', 'Smt. Bharati G.R', and 'Smt. Ramalakshmi I.C' residing in the same locality, observe that 'G.' / 'G.R' / 'I.C' represent family initials / children / wife of 'I.A. Gundappa'.
3. State this hypothesis explicitly: "The vendors appear to be the legal heirs (wife and children) of the previous purchaser (Shri. I.A. Gundappa)."
4. Highlight missing EC evidence: Point out that no registered Legal Heir Certificate, Partition Deed, or Will is recorded in the EC ledger for this transfer.
5. Provide actionable guidance: Advise the user to inspect physical Legal Heir Certificates, the Death Certificate of the deceased owner, and Revenue Mutation (Khata) records to confirm lawful devolution of title before the sale.
6. If Vendors have NO name/initial/residence connection to the prior purchaser, explicitly flag it as an unexplained acquisition gap or competing title chain.

CRITICAL RULE — NO MATCHING PROPERTY:
If NO EC entries match the Sale Deed property (the survey/plot/CTS
numbers and locality are completely different between the SD and ALL
EC entries), return an EMPTY "transactions" array []. Do NOT force
a match. Set "title_story" to: "No transactions registered related
to this property details in the Encumbrance Certificate. The EC may
belong to a different property."

EXCLUDE entries on other properties (different survey number and
locality) — the LLM must not return them. INCLUDE the SD's own entry
with chain_role THE_SD.

STEP 4 — TITLE STORY (COMPREHENSIVE MULTI-PARAGRAPH AUDIT).
Do NOT summarize in 3-5 lines. Write a detailed, multi-paragraph plain-language narrative story for "title_story" covering:

Paragraph 1 — CHRONOLOGICAL TITLE FLOW: Trace every transaction in sequence from the root/earliest entry to the latest entry. For each step, state the date, registration reference, parties (Vendor -> Purchaser), property portion, and nature of conveyance.

Paragraph 2 — ACQUISITION LINKS & GAPS (INTELLECTUAL AUDIT): Detail how each vendor acquired title. Explicitly explain any missing predecessor deeds, unexplained acquisition gaps, or implied legal heir transfers (mentioning party names, matching initials/family relationships, and missing registered inheritance documents). State what offline documents (Legal Heir Certificate, Death Certificate, Mutation entries) the user must inspect.

Paragraph 3 — OWNERSHIP VERDICT & ACTIVE ENCUMBRANCES: Explicitly clarify whether the purchaser named in the uploaded Sale Deed currently retains ownership of record, or if ownership has moved to subsequent buyers. Detail all active encumbrances (e.g. active bank mortgages / Deposit of Title Deeds) against the current owners.

Paragraph 4 — EC RECENCY & SEARCH RANGE RECOMMENDATION: Check the latest transaction date / search end date in the EC. If the EC search range ends prior to the present date, warn the user and recommend fetching an updated Encumbrance Certificate (up to the current date) to ensure no recent unrecorded transactions or mortgages exist.
