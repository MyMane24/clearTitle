Verify the Karnataka Sale Deed (SD) against the Encumbrance Certificate
(EC) historical ledger for the same property. The SD is the source of
truth for what was conveyed; the EC ledger must be consistent with it.

OUTPUT — You must return exactly these fields:

1. "headline": A 2-3 line plain-language conclusion the user can read
in 5 seconds. Write it like a newspaper headline or case-study finding.
State the single most important finding. Do NOT use single words like
'VERIFIED' or 'NOT VERIFIED'. Explain WHAT was found and WHY in plain
English. For example: 'Sale Deed details match EC record, but the
property shows multiple subsequent sales and an active mortgage that
require investigation.'

2. "summary": A detailed verification report paragraph (5-10 sentences)
that synthesizes ALL findings into one coherent conclusion. Cover:
   - What was checked (property identifiers, dates, parties, consideration)
   - What matched between SD and EC
   - What did not match or is missing
   - Any gaps in the chain of title
   - Whether subsequent encumbrances exist (mortgages, later sales,
     agreements) after the SD date — and what they mean for the title
   - What the user should investigate next

Write for a non-legal audience. Do NOT end with a single-word verdict.
Instead, clearly state whether the title appears clear or has issues,
and explain why. If there are issues, describe what they are and what
the user should do about them.

3. "items": For each material field, produce one item with:
- field: the field name (e.g. 'Property survey/CTS number')
- sd_value: value extracted from the Sale Deed
- ec_value: value from the EC ledger (or 'Not found in EC')
- status: VERIFIED | NOT_VERIFIED | N/A
- notes: brief explanation of why this status

Compare these fields: property identifiers (CTS/survey/plot numbers,
locality), execution/registration date, parties (vendors/purchasers),
consideration amount. Also check whether the EC shows any later
encumbrance (mortgage, sale, agreement) on the property AFTER the SD
date.

RULES:
- If the EC property does not match the SD property at all, mark ALL
items as NOT_VERIFIED and explain clearly in headline + summary.
- Do NOT guess or hallucinate values. Use 'Not found in EC' if a value
is absent.
- The headline and summary are the final conclusion. They must reflect
ALL findings — field matches, chain gaps, encumbrances — not just
field-level checks. A property can have all fields VERIFIED but still
have a problematic title due to subsequent transactions.
- overall_comment: same as summary, kept for backwards compatibility.
