STAGE: CONSOLIDATE PERSISTED DOSSIER FINDINGS

The application consolidates typed findings from every transaction dossier into
one property-level findings container. Two deterministic inputs follow this
text: CANDIDATE FINDINGS (identical observations already collapsed; `sources`
lists every transaction/position that observed them) and OPEN EVIDENCE
REQUIREMENTS (evidence the case still awaits).

`PropertyFinding` in `backend/schemas/analysis.py` is the output contract; the
application validates it, assigns finding IDs, transaction coverage, document
references, and provenance. You propose only what is modeled below.

For each returned draft under `findings`:
- `members`: indexes from CANDIDATE FINDINGS covered by the group (>= 1).
- `category`: one value from the schema's category list; `title`: a short
  property-level statement; `description`: merged factually — reuse the
  members' own content only. Never re-investigate; never add a fact, date,
  amount, or document you were not given.
- `severity`: the schema's enum. CRITICAL sits behind an application gate: it
  requires sourced document evidence across members and a defect-grounded
  `implication`. "Document asked, not examined" alone is never a defect: at
  most CAUTION, framed as an evidence gap. POSITIVE for defect-free
  established positions; INFORMATION for neutral records.
- `confidence`: HIGH only when all members are ESTABLISHED with consistent
  direct evidence; medium is the safe default.
- `implication`: why the finding matters, grounded in sourced facts.
- `required_customer_action`: only when the customer can actually act on it.
- `evidence_gaps`: open-evidence notes directly tied to this group; the
  application adds the affected transactions' OPEN requirement ids itself.

CONTRADICTIONS: when two or more candidates assert incompatible positions
(conflicting survey numbers, areas, succession facts, duplicate chains),
return entries under `contradictions` with `members` naming the conflicting
candidate indexes (>= 2), one factual `description` naming what conflicts,
and `resolution_guidance` (how the conflict would be settled) when you can.

`unresolved_dependencies`: requirement_id values from OPEN EVIDENCE
REQUIREMENTS that still hold findings open.

Do not output finding IDs, transaction coverage lists, or document tables —
the application derives them from your `members`.

TIER-C MODEL-MERGE PROPOSALS (additive, propose-only — C2)

When you believe two or more candidates describe ONE underlying issue but the
application's deterministic anchors cannot establish it (different observed
forms of the same parcel, parties recorded only in prose), you may still return
one draft grouping their `members`. This is a PROPOSAL: the application runs
seven checks on it and rejects any proposal that fails one — rejected groups
are split back into single findings automatically, with the rejection recorded.

Your proposal is accepted only when it satisfies, on the members as given:
1. one category, exactly;
2. no exclusionary conflict — different `defect_kind` signals, an
   equal instrument recorded at different offices, opposing party roles for
   the same party, opposite defect polarity, or unrelated parcel identifiers;
   a `generic` kind never merges;
3. party-role compatibility — agreeing transferor/transferee patterns across
   members support you; missing roles never support;
4. consistent legal polarity on `is_legal_defect`;
5. at least one supporting signal: an agreeing party role held by two or more
   members, chronologically adjacent chain positions among the members'
   transactions, or overlapping affected parties;
6. no candidate in the group appears in a `contradictions` entry (a
   contradicted pair is never unified);
7. the members' normalized title/text tokens overlap by at least one third.

State the shared anchors you rely on in the merged `description`, reusing only
the members' own content. If any check is unclear from the data, do not group —
return separate drafts. The application stays the sole authority for identity,
IDs, statuses, and ordering; your prose never invents one.

RELATED-PAIR PROPOSALS (additive, propose-only — OD-4 extension)

You may propose cross-finding relations the deterministic rules cannot derive:
`related_pairs`, a list of `[indexA, indexB]` pairs naming two CANDIDATE
FINDINGS indexes that address the same subject matter without sharing
identity (an old registration issue and the correcting deed that time-bars
it; a disputed survey recital and the plot record that contradicts only its
edge). Rules:
- RELATED semantics only: neither finding contradicts the other and neither
  supersedes the other — genuinely distinct issues about one subject.
- Never propose a pair you also listed in `contradictions` (that record
  already carries CONFLICTS_WITH edges).
- At most `[indexA, indexB]` two-element pairs over valid candidate indexes;
  the application re-validates every pair against its own output map —
  proposals that fail validation are dropped and recorded. A proposal never
  merges findings, rewires identity, or affects statuses.
